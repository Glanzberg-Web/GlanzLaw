Mediation & Dispute Resolution

Resolution with the litigation record in view.

Jack Glanzberg, Esq. publicly identifies himself as a certified mediator since October 2022 and has longstanding experience litigating insurance and property disputes.

Why mediate

A structured setting to test positions and explore settlement.

Mediation does not require parties to abandon their legal positions. It creates a confidential process for evaluating risk, exchanging information, and exploring whether a negotiated outcome is possible.

For disputes involving insurance, property loss, contractors, or commercial relationships, the underlying record can be technical and the costs of continued litigation can grow quickly. A mediator familiar with litigation can help organize the discussion around the issues that are likely to matter most.

Insurance & coverage

Policy interpretation, property claims, indemnification, and related coverage disputes.

Property disputes

Property damage, construction-related losses, and responsibility for repair or replacement.

Civil litigation

Contractor negligence, premises matters, and related commercial or tort disputes.

Pre-suit or pending case

Mediation may be useful before filing or after litigation has clarified the parties’ positions.

Process

What a mediation can look like.

The exact format should fit the dispute, the parties, and the court or private process involved.

01

Preparation

Identify the core legal and factual issues, the relevant documents, and the decision-makers who need to participate.

02

Initial session

Set the ground rules, clarify the issues, and give each side an opportunity to explain the dispute and its priorities.

03

Focused negotiation

Use joint or separate sessions as appropriate to test assumptions, identify risk, and explore potential terms.

04

Resolution or next steps

If an agreement is reached, the parties can memorialize the material terms. If not, the process can still narrow issues and clarify what remains disputed.
